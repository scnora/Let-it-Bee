Raw Honey is Un-bee-liveable

By Sarai Ojeda | March 29, 2026  |  Rating: 5/5

image by Sarai Ojeda

Ever wonder why people decide to buy local honey rather than store-bought?

Well, there are many reasons why! Honey has been used as a remedy for many issues across history. There is much research that implies that raw honey has many health benefits and medical uses. This is why many tend to turn to raw honey as opposed to store-bought. When you buy store-bought honey, it is very filtered and pasteurized, stripping a lot of the beneficial nutrients and antibacterial properties you'd find in raw pure honey.

One of the biggest benefits of raw honey is its high antioxidant content. These antioxidants help protect your body from damage caused by free radicals, which are linked to aging and diseases like heart disease and cancer. Because raw honey isn't exposed to high heat during pasteurization, it is able to retain more of these helpful compounds. In contrast, store-bought honey often goes through heating and filtering processes that reduce its overall nutritional value.

Another important benefit of raw honey is its natural antibacterial and antifungal properties. For years, honey has been used as a home remedy for soothing sore throats, calming coughs, and even helping wounds heal. Raw honey is especially effective in this way because it still contains the enzymes and compounds that give it these healing properties. When honey is processed, many of these beneficial elements are either reduced or completely removed, making it less effective for these uses.

Unlike Raw Honey, store-bought honey is often highly filtered to improve its appearance and processed to extend its shelf life, but this can remove important nutrients like pollen. Additionally, processed honey may even contain added sugars or syrups. Local Honey businesses filter by uncapping honeycomb frames, extracting the honey using a honey extractor or other methods, and use a sieve or mesh strainer to remove excess wax or pollen.

Despite raw honey having multiple benefits, there are some downsides. It is not recommended for infants — this can cause infant botulism if raw honey contains Clostridium botulinum spores. It is also not recommended for people who have a weak immune system. Make sure to buy raw honey from reputable beekeepers and store it in an airtight container.

Overall, raw honey is considered a better option than store-bought because it maintains more of its natural nutrients, antioxidants, and healing properties.

Is BeeKeeping Worth it? : Review for Beginners

By Sarai Ojeda | March 29, 2026  |  Rating: 3/5

image by Sarai Ojeda

Have you ever wondered if beekeeping is something you'd want to get into? A lot of people like the idea of beekeeping for many reasons, and I was too. It seemed like a peaceful and relaxed hobby, but once you start looking more into it and all that it entails, there's a lot more involved than you might expect.

One of the biggest reasons people start beekeeping is for the benefits. Of course, the obvious one is honey. Having your own fresh, raw honey feels rewarding and tastes way better than anything store-bought. You can also make your own candles, soaps, lip balm and beer! On top of that, bees help with pollination, which can improve gardens and crops significantly. Some people just want to help the environment and support declining bee populations.

That being said, beekeeping definitely has its downsides. Starting a single hive can cost anywhere from about $400 to $650 when you include equipment and bees. You also have to buy protective gear, tools (smoker, hive tools, etc.), and possibly honey extraction equipment. Maintenance costs like feeding, treatments, and repairs can run anywhere from $150 to $500 per year.

Another thing to consider is the time and effort. Beekeeping isn't something you can just set up and ignore. You have to regularly check your hive, watch for diseases or Varroa mites, and make sure your bees are healthy. There's also a learning curve, especially for beginners. And of course, getting stung is part of the experience.

I've also heard from people who've actually tried it, and their experiences are pretty mixed. Some say it becomes a really fulfilling hobby over time, especially once you start harvesting honey or even selling it. Others say it takes a few years just to break even.

There are also restrictions depending on where you live. Certain towns or neighborhoods have rules about keeping bees, so check local regulations before starting.

Overall, beekeeping can definitely be worth it, but it depends on what you're looking for. If you want a relaxing, rewarding hobby and don't mind the cost and effort, it can be an amazing experience.
